# Roadmap research note M6: Waste-picker recognition

Status: ROADMAP (research note only, nothing built, **no waste-picker figure is shown anywhere in the app**). Owner: Likitha. Written 2026-11-11.

## Who and why
Informal waste pickers and collectors do much of India's door-to-door collection and sorting, often without secure pay, protection or recognition. A resident, councillor or reporter asking "who actually collects my street's waste, and are they recognised?" gets no answer from the official waste figures we hold, which count tonnes and not people. This note records what is known, what is disputed and what we cannot say, so nobody on the team repeats a number we cannot source.

## What a first search found (not yet read in full)
The points below come from web search results on 2026-11-11. I read the search summaries, not the full pages or the rule text, so each point is a lead to verify and not a finding to publish.
- **The Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026** (in force from 1 April 2026 according to several explainers) and waste pickers: **sources disagree.** The Wire's coverage says the rules make integrating waste pickers or informal collectors into mainstream waste management a duty of urban local bodies. A Centre for Science and Environment commentator quoted by Down To Earth says several recommendations, including provisions for informal waste pickers, were not explicitly addressed. The notified text must be read to settle this. Even the notification date differs between explainers (27 or 28 January 2026).
- **The 2016 Rules** mentioned waste pickers and asked urban local bodies for identity cards. Down To Earth reports that recognition rarely turned into secure livelihoods, safety or a say in decisions, and another source says most urban local bodies did not issue the cards (budget or reluctance). Both are one outlet's account each.
- **How many?** There is **no single official national count** in what I found. Estimates are rough and conflict (for example 1.5 to 4 million in one source, "more than 4 million" in another, 15 to 20 lakh in an explainer). A government profiling slide deck shared by CSE reports about 91,000 waste pickers and workers surveyed in 700-plus urban local bodies and about 42,000 validated records, but it carries no date. These numbers measure different things (an estimate of everyone, versus people enumerated so far).
- **Schemes and models:** MoHUA's NAMASTE scheme was reported as adding occupation-based ID cards and a registration app for waste pickers, with Pune first (per an international waste-picker alliance, an advocacy group). The SWaCH cooperative in Pune is repeatedly cited as a model that integrates waste pickers into door-to-door collection.

## What we do not have
- The notified rule text, read and quoted with clause numbers.
- Any state-wise or city-wise count of waste pickers from an official document we have opened.
- Any data on pay, safety, or how many pickers hold identity cards.

## A version we could build later
Not a number. A short, sourced explainer page ("Who collects your waste?") that states what the rules say (once the text is read and quoted), names the schemes with their official pages, and links to organisations of waste pickers, with each statement tied to a document and page like every other figure. Possibly a prompt in the ward and complaint tools to ask who collects waste in the ward, as a free-text note that is never counted or published.

## What we would not claim
- Any count of waste pickers, nationally or locally, unless it is quoted from an official document we hold, with its year and what it counted.
- That the 2026 Rules do or do not protect waste pickers, until the notified text is read.
- That any scheme reached any number of people, beyond what its own official release states.
- Anything about an individual's work, pay or status. We never collect names or identity details.

## Before building
1. Read the notified Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026 and quote, with clause numbers, what it says about informal waste pickers and collectors. Settle the disagreement above.
2. Open the official MoHUA pages for NAMASTE and the profiling exercise, and record exactly what they count and when.
3. Ask a waste-pickers' organisation what a resident-facing page should and should not say, and whether they want to be named at all.
